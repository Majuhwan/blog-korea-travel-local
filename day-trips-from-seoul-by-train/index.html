Day Trips From Seoul You Can Actually Do by Train

What Korea's official rail network actually offers for a one-day trip from Seoul, based on Korea Tourism Organization and rail route information.

Korea Travel, Local ViewOct 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Seoul's train network reaches far enough that a day trip to another city or region is genuinely realistic without an overnight stay, as long as you know which lines actually move fast enough to make it worthwhile. The Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), the country's official tourism body, publishes the route and speed details that make this easy to plan around.

How fast the high-speed lines actually are

According to KTO's official train information, Korea's KTX high-speed trains run at a maximum speed of 330 km/h, and the SRT (Super Rapid Train) runs at up to 300 km/h. The headline route, the Gyeongbu Line connecting Seoul and Busan, covers 447.1 kilometers and carries the largest number of passengers of any route in the country, according to KTO.

Running the numbers on that distance at the KTX's maximum speed gives a theoretical floor for the trip: 447.1 km divided by 330 km/h works out to about 1 hour 21 minutes if a train ran the entire distance at top speed without stopping. In practice, scheduled KTX journeys on this route take noticeably longer than that theoretical floor, since trains don't travel the entire route at maximum speed and typically make intermediate stops. The theoretical number is useful mainly as a ceiling: it tells you the absolute fastest this specific distance could be covered, which helps you judge how much of the real travel time is stops and acceleration versus pure distance.

Three lines KTO highlights, and what's reachable on each

KTO's official train guide describes three major high-speed lines radiating from Seoul:

  • The Gyeongbu Line runs to Busan, Korea's second-largest city, and passes through culture-rich stops including Daejeon and Gyeongju along the way, which KTO specifically recommends for a traveler planning a short but substantial trip.
  • The Honam Line, 252.5 kilometers, runs from Seodaejeon to Mokpo, connecting Seoul to the Jeolla region (also called Honam), with major stops at Iksan, Jeongeup, Gwangju-Songjeong, Naju, and Mokpo. KTO notes Jeongeup Station specifically draws hikers in fall for the autumn foliage at nearby Naejangsan Mountain.
  • The Gangneung Line, 237.7 kilometers, runs from Cheongnyangni Station in Seoul out to Gangneung on the East Sea coast, with stops including Yangpyeong, Seowonju, and Pyeongchang, and is KTO's recommended route for travelers wanting to reach the East Coast's coastline across any season.

Themed tourist trains, if you want the journey itself to be the destination

Beyond the high-speed commuter-style lines, KTO's official guide also describes a separate category of themed tourist trains run through Korail, each designed as a scenic experience rather than pure point-to-point transport. A few examples directly from KTO's listing:

  • The Baekdu-daegan Valley Train (V-Train) runs from Cheoram Station to Yeongju Station, taking about 2 hours 10 minutes through canyon scenery in the Baekdudaegan mountain range, described by KTO as the most rugged mountain range in Korea.
  • The Jeongseon Arirang Train (A-Train) runs between Jecheon Station and Auraji Station, about 2 hours, through the area KTO describes as the origin of the Jeongseon Arirang folk song tradition.
  • The South Coast Train (S-Train) runs two separate routes, one from Seoul to Yeosu EXPO Station (about 4 hours 50 minutes) and one from Busan to Mokpo (about 4 hours 40 minutes), both significantly longer than the high-speed lines and better suited to travelers who want the slower coastal scenery as the point of the trip rather than a quick turnaround.

These tourist trains are long enough in several cases (near 5 hours each way) that they function better as a full-day or overnight excursion than a quick there-and-back trip, which is worth factoring in before committing to one over a straightforward KTX or SRT ride.

How to actually book

KTO's official guidance lists several booking channels: a ticket office or ticket window at the station, the Korail website, the KORAIL+ app, ticketing kiosks, or third-party apps including NAVER Train Booking and KakaoT. Tickets generally go on sale starting one month before departure, from 07:00 until as late as 20 minutes before departure at a station window, or right up to departure time through some apps. KTO also flags the Korail Pass, a multi-day flexible pass built specifically for international travelers, available in 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-day versions usable within a 10-day window, which can be worth considering if a single trip is part of a longer multi-city itinerary rather than a standalone day out.

Key takeaways

  • KTX trains run up to 330 km/h and SRT up to 300 km/h, per KTO's official figures; the Seoul-Busan Gyeongbu Line covers 447.1 km, the single busiest route in the country.
  • Three major high-speed lines from Seoul reach Busan/Daejeon/Gyeongju (Gyeongbu), the Jeolla region (Honam), and the East Sea coast including Gangneung (Gangneung Line).
  • KTO's separately listed themed tourist trains, like the V-Train and A-Train, are scenic rather than fast, and some, like the S-Train, run close to 5 hours each way, better suited to a full-day or overnight trip.
  • Tickets can be booked at station counters, through the Korail website or app, at kiosks, or via NAVER Train Booking and KakaoT, generally from one month ahead.
  • The Korail Pass offers flexible multi-day unlimited travel for international visitors and is worth comparing against single tickets if a day trip is part of a longer Korea itinerary.

Checking a specific line's real scheduled time, not just the theoretical top-speed number, is the only way to know whether a given city is realistically a day trip or better treated as an overnight stop.
