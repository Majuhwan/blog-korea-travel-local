Do You Need a K-ETA for Korea? The 2026 Rules Explained

Who needs a K-ETA to visit Korea, which 22 countries are exempt through December 31, 2026, what it costs, and how long approval takes.

Korea Travel, Local ViewOct 11, 2026 · 4 min read

If you are flying to Korea without a visa, the first question to settle is whether you need a K-ETA, the Korea Electronic Travel Authorization. The answer depends on your passport and on your travel dates, because a temporary exemption that covers many common passports runs only until the end of 2026. Here is how the rules work, using the official K-ETA site run by Korea's Ministry of Justice and the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO).

What a K-ETA is, and what it is not

A K-ETA is an online travel authorization for people from countries and regions in Korea's visa waiver program. In other words, it applies to travelers who do not need a visa. If your nationality requires a visa, a K-ETA does not replace it, and you apply for the visa instead.

The official guide is clear on two limits:

  • It is not a visa. It covers only part of the requirements for visa-free entry.
  • It does not guarantee entry. A Korea Immigration Service officer at the port of entry makes the final decision.

The K-ETA must be approved before you board your flight or ship. The guide warns that applying after boarding, or while already in Korea, can lead to refusal of the K-ETA or entry restrictions.

The temporary exemption, and who it covers

Korea has paused the K-ETA requirement for a list of countries and regions to support tourism. The Ministry of Justice notice extends this exemption from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026 (Korea time).

KTO lists the exempt countries and regions as:

Region Countries and regions
Asia Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Macau
North America United States (including Guam), Canada
Europe United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Belgium, Denmark, Austria
Oceania Australia, New Zealand

That is 22 countries and regions in total. If your passport is on this list and your trip falls within 2026, you can enter visa-free without applying for a K-ETA. When you scan your passport photo page during the application, the site shows a pop-up if you are exempt, so the system itself confirms your status.

Watch the end date. The exemption is tied to a date, not to your booking. If your trip runs past December 31, 2026, or you are planning travel for 2027, check the K-ETA site again before you fly, since the notice does not cover dates after that.

Why some exempt travelers still apply

Being exempt does not stop you from applying. The Ministry of Justice notice says exempt travelers may still apply to get the benefits of an approved K-ETA, such as not having to submit an arrival card. The application guide confirms that K-ETA holders are exempt from the arrival card at entry, which makes immigration more convenient. If you choose this route, the normal fee applies.

Cost, processing time, and validity

From the official application guide:

  • Fee: 10,000 won (the site estimates about USD 7 to 8), with an online payment fee charged separately. The fee is non-refundable, even if the application is denied.
  • Processing: Assessment usually takes 72 hours or less, but it can take longer when applications spike. Applications are reviewed in order, and there is no expedited service.
  • Validity: 3 years, or until your passport expires if that comes first. Applications submitted before July 3, 2023 were valid for 2 years.
  • Multiple use: You can use the same K-ETA for several trips within its validity period.

A practical timing rule follows from the 72-hour figure: apply at least three full days before departure, and earlier if you can, because there is no way to speed up a slow review.

Validity is not the same as length of stay

This point trips people up. A 3-year K-ETA does not let you stay in Korea for 3 years. The guide says the K-ETA validity is separate from the visa-free stay period, and you cannot stay longer than the visa-free period set for your country on each entry. KTO adds that this stay period varies by nationality and points travelers to the Korea Visa Portal to check theirs.

When an existing K-ETA stops working

Even before it expires, a K-ETA needs attention in a few situations. According to the application guide:

  • New passport: If your passport is reissued, you must apply for a new K-ETA.
  • Changed personal records: Changes to your criminal record or infectious disease information also require a new application.
  • Changed travel details: If your purpose of entry, address of stay, contact details, arrival or departure dates, or companions change, update them on the K-ETA site's Update Travel Information page. Not doing so can lead to entry denial.

The guide also warns that false or inaccurate information can get an approval refused or canceled, with possible penalties, and that this applies to everyone on the same application.

How to apply

Apply only on the official site, k-eta.go.kr. The guide lists the steps as agreeing to the terms, signing up or verifying your email, entering passport information, entering application details, checking the information, paying, and completing the application. Members and non-members follow the same seven steps, except that non-members verify an email address instead of creating an account.

If you have questions about visas in general, KTO lists the Immigration Contact Center at 1345 inside Korea, or +82-2-1345 from abroad, on weekdays from 09:00 to 22:00.

Key takeaways

  • A K-ETA is for visa-free travelers only, must be approved before boarding, and does not guarantee entry.
  • 22 countries and regions, including the US, Canada, the UK, Japan, and Australia, are exempt through December 31, 2026.
  • Exempt travelers can still apply to skip the arrival card, at the normal 10,000 won fee.
  • Allow at least 72 hours for review, since there is no expedited option.
  • A K-ETA lasts 3 years or until your passport expires, but each stay is still limited to your country's visa-free period.
  • A new passport means a new K-ETA application.
